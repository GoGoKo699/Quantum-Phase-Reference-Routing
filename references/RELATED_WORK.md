# Related work

| Work | Connection |
| --- | --- |
| [Wiseman and Vaccaro (2003)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.91.097902) and [Bartlett, Rudolph and Spekkens (2007)](https://doi.org/10.1103/RevModPhys.79.555) | Local number superselection, activation of shared single excitations and fixed-number encodings supply the physical framework. |
| [Abiuso et al. (2022)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.01726) and [Renou and Beigi (2022)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.00905) | Single-excitation links and coherent orientation alternatives support photon-counting or token-counting network statistics. Abiuso et al., Supplement E, treats the ring with the same elementary edge state. Those protocols produce measurement statistics for network nonlocality. |
| [Chin, Kim and Lee (2021)](https://doi.org/10.22331/q-2021-12-23-611) | Perfect matchings and cycles organize postselected optical states, including GHZ and Dicke constructions. |
| [Gioan (2007)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejc.2005.11.006) | Prescribed indegrees and cycle reversals are established graph-theoretic tools. |
| [Acín, Cirac and Lewenstein (2007)](https://doi.org/10.1038/nphys549) | Heralded pair conversion and swapping connect quantum network routing to classical percolation. Their model supplies pure entangled links with local pair conversion. The present network first creates an encoded pair from a multi-edge block whose individual links are separable after twirling. |
| [Kesten (1980)](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01197577) and [Burton and Keane (1989)](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01217761) | The square-lattice bond threshold and uniqueness of the infinite cluster underlie the ideal architecture. |
| [Vidal (1999)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.83.1046) | Single-copy pure-state conversion gives the bipartite probability bound used in the strict graph example and the complete bipartite graph calculation. |
| [Cooper and Frieze (2004)](https://doi.org/10.1017/S096354830400611X) | Their directed degree-sequence model relates strong connectivity to branching. The present graph calculation uses separate direction-compatible matchings between the two bipartite color classes. |
| [McKay, Wormald and Wysocka (2004)](https://doi.org/10.37236/1819) | Their short-cycle law supplies the probability of simplicity in the bipartite configuration model. |
| [Brito, Dumitriu and Harris (2022)](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0963548321000249) | Their spectral gap for random bipartite biregular graphs supplies the constant edge expansion used to find short edge-disjoint routes. |
| [Romancino et al. (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10189v2) | Their random-network percolation model starts with independently convertible partially entangled links. Here individual raw links are separable after twirling, and local measurements exploit coherence within network charge sectors. |

[Entanglement Valence Threshold](https://github.com/GoGoKo699/Entanglement-Valence-Threshold/tree/e3217a7babf8448c77799747623294c3c05d8357) supplies the same node-dephased link model on bipartite graphs, its charge-fiber representation, the directed-cycle argument, and two-terminal extraction on a single cycle. Its principal entanglement quantity permits quantum operations within whole color classes. Here each vertex is a separate laboratory, with specified individual terminals and exact one-batch success as the objective. The [degree-four comparison](../science/degree-four-routing.md#a-separation-from-color-coalition-entanglement) gives a common input with distinct scaling under these access rules.

[Quantum Timestamped Entanglement](https://github.com/GoGoKo699/Quantum-Timestamped-Entanglement/tree/4a71a2c85442b260880caa9f7f1605f0fa5b266b) analyzes a two-laboratory receiver and stream with temporal phase drift and false heralds. The shared ingredient is excitation-count projection. The parallel-path law concerns spatial routing with intermediate nodes and a common phase action at each node.

The scientific additions studied here are two-terminal sector localizability, the attained probability law for pooling internally disjoint paths, a bounded-degree architecture using those pooled gadgets, and a separate analysis of sparse random four-regular graphs and charge-adaptive circuits.
